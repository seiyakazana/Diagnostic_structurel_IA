# PRD Quality Review — Diagnostic Structurel IA (2026-07-23, finalized 2026-07-27)

## Overall verdict
This PRD holds up as the scope contract for its downstream chain: the thesis ("compress ~85% deskwork into a review-and-sign step; the AI proposes, the Expert disposes") is stated once and enforced everywhere — in Non-Goals, in FR-13's "No unvalidated Détection can appear in a Rapport", and in counter-metric SM-C1. Scope honesty and traceability are unusually clean (11/11 assumption roundtrip, contiguous FR-1–FR-24, every FR mapped to a UJ and SM). What's at risk is the tail of the pipeline it bets everything on: FR-18 (offline Rapport generation) is the most architecturally consequential FR and ships with zero testable consequences, and the acceptance surface for AI detection outside fissures/corrosion is undefined — both will surface as ambiguity in story creation, exactly where this PRD is otherwise strongest.

## Decision-readiness — strong
Decisions are stated as decisions, with what was given up named: bounding boxes over segmentation (FR-11, §7.2), no in-app signature (§6), RGPD deferred entirely with a hard gate ("This is a hard gate before any external user or commercial pilot", §7.2), and "No claimed AI moat" (§6) — a rare piece of self-honesty that removes a whole class of downstream overpromising. Open Questions (§10) are genuinely open, with owners and revisit triggers (Q3 "revisit at SM-7 gate", Q7 "at PRD sign-off"). The two `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts sit at the real tensions (arrêté template absorption at FR-19; RGPD gate at §7.2), not at safe checkpoints.

### Findings
- **low** SM-1 date commitment precedes the timeline decision (§8 SM-1 vs §10 Q7) — SM-1 targets "~1.5 days … by end of 2026" while Q7 says timeline and budget are set only at PRD sign-off. A calendar-dated success metric before any timeline exists is a soft contradiction. *Fix:* phrase SM-1's gate relative to pilot missions completed ("within N pilot Missions") or explicitly mark the date provisional pending Q7.

## Substance over theater — strong
Almost nothing here is furniture. The two personas are the two actual founders, honestly tagged (`[ASSUMPTION: persona name — the associate/founding Expert]`), and they drive real requirements: Marc's "he wears gloves; one hand often holds a lamp" (UJ-1) is directly load-bearing for the §9 field-ergonomics NFRs (≤3 taps, ≥14pt, one-handed operation). NFRs carry product-specific bounds (200 ms local entry, 30 s sync, ≤3 taps) rather than "scalable/secure/reliable" boilerplate. The Vision (§1) could not be swapped into another PRD — it names Coupes, Schémas, and the 3.5-day baseline. Competitive claims in §2 are backed by the addendum's named-vendor research rather than asserted.

### Findings
- **low** Ergonomics list mixes bounded and unbounded items (§9 Field ergonomics) — "haptic feedback on important actions", "explicit icons", "dark mode" sit unbounded next to testable thresholds. Justified by the field context, but story creation will have to invent done-ness for them. *Fix:* mark these as UX-spec-owned qualities so the downstream UX workflow knows it owns their definition.

## Strategic coherence — strong
The PRD has one thesis and every feature group serves the single pipeline Relevé → Détection → Validation → Coupes → Rapport. Prioritization follows the thesis: SM-1 is explicitly "the near-term proof gate: *does it cut the founder's own deskwork in half, on real missions?*" — the right metric for a founders-first POC, not activity theater. Counter-metrics exist and are the correct ones: SM-C1 (rubber-stamp rate as automation-complacency red flag) and SM-C2 (compliance floor). MVP scope kind is a coherent problem-solving POC, and §3.2 Non-Users plus §6 keep it that way.

### Findings
- **medium** FR-23 exceeds what the POC thesis needs (§5.7 FR-23) — "precision/recall/F1 per Désordre category, plus model version history and comparison, and receives performance-drift alerts" is MLOps machinery for a two-founder pilot with a manually exported dataset (FR-22) and unquantified retraining cadence (§10 Q5). Drift alerts are also undefined (no threshold, no baseline), so the FR fails done-ness too. Build effort here competes directly with the SM-1 proof gate. *Fix:* keep per-category precision from Validation outcomes as v1; defer version comparison and drift alerts to v2 or tag them `[NON-GOAL for MVP]`.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
The strongest FRs are genuinely testable — FR-1, FR-5 (200 ms), FR-7 (30 s sync, force-close survival), FR-11 (>75% precision), FR-13 (<5 s per Détection, "No unvalidated Détection can appear in a Rapport") — and the cahier §9.x acceptance citations are consistently restated as consequences, as §0 promises. But coverage is uneven: 11 of 24 FRs carry no Consequences block (FR-3, FR-6, FR-8, FR-9, FR-10, FR-16, FR-18, FR-20, FR-21, FR-23, FR-24), and the gaps cluster on the Rapport tail and the drawing tools — the deskwork-elimination features the thesis rests on. Since this PRD sits chain-top above epics and stories, this is the dimension that will leak ambiguity downstream.

### Findings
- **high** FR-18 has zero testable consequences on the most complex behavior in the PRD (§5.6 FR-18) — "the on-device render assembles all locally captured data, and AI-derived content … integrates when the PDF is finalized at sync" defines two artifacts (offline render, sync-finalized PDF) without saying which is authoritative, whether the offline render is exportable/shareable via FR-20, or how the Expert knows a Rapport is provisional vs final. An engineer cannot know what "done" is; an architect must guess the sync semantics. *Fix:* add consequences: the offline render is visibly marked provisional and cannot be shared via FR-20; the finalized PDF supersedes it; finalization occurs automatically at sync.
- **medium** The "content floor" of two of the three Rapport templates is never specified (§5.6 FR-17) — the rapport de diagnostic complet is fully enumerated, but rapport de visite and fiche de désordre unitaire get only "their template's deliberately partial content floor", which is defined nowhere in PRD or addendum. Two of three v1 deliverables have no content spec. *Fix:* enumerate each template's minimum content in the addendum, or add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` delegating the floor to the UX/template spec.
- **medium** No acceptance bar for four of six Détection categories (§5.4 FR-11, §8 SM-3) — precision >75% applies to fissures and corrosion only (per cahier §9.2), but FR-11 requires classification "into the six categories and their sub-disorders". Whether v1 ships if humidité/déformations/maçonnerie/dégradations detection is poor is unstated. *Fix:* state explicitly that the other four categories are best-effort in v1 with no acceptance threshold, or set one.
- **medium** FR-8 geolocation has no failure behavior in the exact setting UJ-1 describes (§5.2 FR-8) — "Each survey entry and photo is automatically stamped with geolocation" is unconditional, yet UJ-1's climax is a basement survey where GPS, like network, is unavailable. Does capture block, stamp last-known-fix, or stamp null? *Fix:* one consequence: when no fix is available, capture proceeds and the entry is stamped with last-known location flagged as approximate.
- **low** Drawing/annotation tools lack done criteria (FR-10, FR-16) — "arrows, circles, and text" and "freehand strokes, geometric shapes, legends, and dimensions (cotations), with Bluetooth stylus support" enumerate capabilities but define no verifiable outcome. *Fix:* add one round-trip consequence tying annotations/Schémas to their legible appearance in the exported PDF.

## Scope honesty — strong
This is the PRD's best dimension. §6 Non-Goals does real work — each entry closes a door someone would otherwise assume open ("No ouvrages d'art … including as a form option"; "No signature. … No in-app signature, drawn or certificate-based"). §7.2 de-scopes explicitly rather than silently (segmentation masks, retraining pipeline, RGPD, integrations). Eleven `[ASSUMPTION]` tags mark genuine inferences (200 ms threshold, bounding boxes, secure-link mechanism) and all are indexed. Open-items density is well matched to POC stakes: seven Open Questions, all with owners or triggers, none rhetorical. The Cahier-truncation problem (§10 Q1) is disclosed rather than papered over.

### Findings
- **low** Security/auth expectations for the POC are silent (§9) — RGPD deferral is honestly gated, but nothing states even the POC-level bar: authentication exists (UJ-1 entry state assumes it; FR-24 enforces roles) yet there is no line on credential handling or data-at-rest for photos of third parties' buildings. Defensible for a founders-only pilot, but it should be an explicit deferral, not an absence. *Fix:* one NFR line or a §6/§7.2 entry stating the POC security floor and deferring the rest with the RGPD gate.

## Downstream usability — strong
This is a chain-top PRD and it behaves like one. The Glossary (§4) is prescriptive ("Downstream workflows must use these terms exactly"), keeps French domain vocabulary native, and is used consistently — Détection vs Désordre vs Fiche de désordre never blur, which is the drift that would hurt most. Every FR names the UJ it realizes; every SM names the FRs it validates and the cross-references resolve. Sections extract cleanly: the taxonomy lives once in the Glossary/addendum and is cited, not restated. UJs each carry a named protagonist with context inline.

### Findings
- **low** User documentation has no FR (§7.1, §8 SM-6) — documentation is an MVP deliverable ("sufficient for a new Expert to be productive in under one hour") and SM-6's entire subject, but SM-6 "Validates FR-4, FR-13", neither of which produces documentation. Epic/story generation driven off FRs will drop this deliverable. *Fix:* add a small FR under §5.8 or an explicit deliverable line the epics workflow is told to pick up.

## Shape fit — strong
The shape matches the product: a chain-top POC for two founders, one of whom is the practicing target user. Three UJs is the right count — UJ-1 and UJ-2 are the two halves of the thesis and are written at full journey depth; UJ-3 is deliberately one line, correctly refusing to inflate the back-office role into journey theater. Role modeling (§5.8: "the model exists so the pilot mirrors real cabinet structure") is justified rather than assumed. Brownfield concerns don't apply; regulatory traceability, which this shape does demand, is handled well (décret citations in FR-17 consequences, full depth pushed to the addendum). The only over-formalization pressure is at feature level (FR-23, already flagged under Strategic coherence), not document level.
